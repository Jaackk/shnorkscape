# Combat and companion skills: third refinement pass (950)

Reviewed and implemented 12 September 2026 in `950RevTest`. This pass fills the missing Summon operation and adds ordinary thrown auto-attacks. It extends the existing native action/container/world/XP infrastructure. It does not implement retail EOC combat or every familiar ability.

## Summoning: usable companions

85 ordinary familiar pouches now support their cached Summon inventory operation. A successful summon checks the base Summoning level, available Summoning points, the exact unmodified inventory item, area/controller restrictions, and clear space for the current NPC footprint. It consumes one pouch through the same native container transaction used by other skills. The prepared NPC is removed if the inventory transaction fails. Duplicate summons neither replace an existing companion nor consume another pouch.

The companion follows through the existing `Entity.calcFollow` collision route and the single native NPC movement tick. It is recalled beside its owner after a large separation, a plane change, or repeated blocked routing. Placement checks terrain, other NPCs, other players and the owner's footprint. NPC identity is mapped to one owner: other players cannot use its controls, and companions are not registered as attackable combat targets.

Click the familiar to open the existing verified native dialogue menu: remaining time/points, call, renew, dismiss or close. Renewal requires and consumes another matching pouch plus its normal point cost; renewal and dismissal each have a confirmation. Pouch manufacture and scroll conversion retain their existing 161 supported recipes. At a supported Summoning obelisk, Renew-points now restores the existing Summoning skill point resource instead of opening the pouch recipe list. Renewal and pouch use follow the mechanics described in [Summoning familiars](https://runescape.wiki/w/Summoning_familiars) and [Beasts of Burden](https://runescape.wiki/w/Beasts_of_Burden).

Lifetime values are explicit resource entries, modernized to the 16-minute increments introduced in 2021 and checked against the Wiki familiar table. Examples: Spirit wolf 16, Bunyip 48, Unicorn stallion 64, Nihil 96 and Rune minotaur 144 minutes. Talon beast uses 48 minutes, supported by the [useful items guide](https://runescape.wiki/w/Guide_to_useful_items_and_unlocks); one indexed familiar-table excerpt still showed the older 49. The checked table, including the longer minotaur duration, is [Summoning familiars](https://runescape.wiki/w/Summoning_familiars). All 85 actual chosen values are recorded in `summoning-familiars-950.tsv`; duration is server mechanics data, not claimed to be an item cache field.

Player death preserves the companion, matching the modern change recorded on the Wiki. If the actual NPC dies, is finished, or disappears from the world registry, its state is cleared. Logout removes its world entity after the checkpoint; the saved `{pouchId, remainingTicks}` returns on an eligible world tick without consuming another pouch or points. This implementation pauses duration offline. It also suppresses the NPC and pauses lifetime while its controller disallows Summoning or the owner is in a reserved native Dungeoneering trial; it returns when the owner leaves. The suspension policy is a deliberate local compatibility policy, not a claim that every live-game restriction is reproduced.

Existing Skills owns Summoning points. The original 910 familiar drain cadence is retained: one engine skill-level unit (ten displayed points) per 100 world ticks. Point level is already included in saved skill state. The new lifetime tuple is separate: root integration added it to nested skill progress v3 with old-save migration. Tuple validation is bounded and independent of cache availability; restore rechecks the current pouch/NPC metadata. Caller arrays are cloned.

Bunyip additionally restores 2% of maximum life points every 25 ticks (15 seconds), while nearby and its owner is alive. [Hit points](https://runescape.wiki/w/Hit_points). Other familiar combat, special scrolls, beast-of-burden storage, gathering boosts/foraging, full Summoning panel controls and special energy are not implemented. The management text says so. Dungeon pouches and three renamed demon identities remain excluded instead of guessing their current NPCs. Familiar NPCs are intentionally separate from the legacy `Player.getFamiliar()` object; the native controller suppression closes that compatibility gap without invoking the old familiar packet/timer engine.

## Paired-cache evidence

`resources/native950/summoning-familiars-950.properties` contains SHA-256 pins for all 85 pouch and NPC definitions and explicit durations. Startup through `Native950Summoning.verifyCacheBindings()` validates the new familiar bindings alongside existing production assets.

- Item parameter74 links to the current non-PvP familiar NPC, and NPC parameter75 must link back to the same pouch. Exact current item/NPC names, Summon menu option, ordinary templates, models, size and absence of Attack are checked. For example pouch12047 selects NPC6829; the original 910 enum's NPC6830 is the alternate attackable variant and is deliberately not reused. Bunyip12029 selects6813.
- Item parameter394 supplies the current required level, and396 supplies point cost. The latter is divided by100 for the existing engine's skill-level point unit, then displayed multiplied by10. This uses the paired cache even where an old table differs: current Spirit spider requires level10. The summary TSV columns are pouch ID, NPC ID, name, footprint size, required level, raw point parameter396 and duration minutes.
- No 910 item/NPC definition bytes, interface IDs or packet layouts were copied. Original910 `Summoning.Pouches` provides authored pouch identities; `Familiar` provides lifecycle architecture; current strict cache definitions resolve current assets.

## Ranged: consumable thrown weapons

The generic weapon profile now recognizes current dart, throwing-knife, throwing-axe and javelin structs. They use their own equipped stack as ammunition, respecting cache requirements, tier, speed, reach, attack animation and armour defence. Every committed auto-attack consumes exactly one equipped weapon through `Native950Containers.consumeEquipment`; missing, charged or customized stacks are refused. Ordinary higher-tier darts such as Death Lotus also use this structural policy. This is a family-level change rather than individual item handlers. [Thrown weapons](https://runescape.wiki/w/Thrown_weapons).

The last thrown weapon exposed an underlying XP ordering issue: inventory cost is committed before damage rewards, so classifying the now-empty weapon slot would incorrectly award melee XP. The combat loop now passes its captured loadout to the reward hook; Ranged/Defence use the original selected-skill split and Skills rates. Constitution remains damage-linked. When the equipped thrown stack runs out, the player stops attacking while NPC retaliation continues. Automatic retaliation does not unexpectedly resume with unarmed punches; an explicit new attack can resume with the player's new equipment. The same supply-stop guard avoids repeated automatic attacks without runes/ammunition.

Cache evidence in `combat-thrown-950.tsv` records 15 current item examples and five weapon structs with hashes. Structs14943/14944/14945/14946 use family10; javelins have animation37465, other ordinary throws37463. Positive item parameter2940 is required. Chinchompas have a distinct14947 struct, and the deathtouched dart lacks the ordinary ammunition parameter. Legacy poisoned variants (parameter31) are refused. Chinchompa area damage, instant kills, poison, powered/degrading weapons, offhand attacks, ammunition recovery and rendered projectiles remain separate work. These distinctions follow [Ranged weapons](https://runescape.wiki/w/Weapon/Ranged_weapons), while damage retains this project's conservative classic formula.

## Other owned skill review

Prayer retains the second-pass bone/ash offering and altar recharge implementation. Slayer retains saved tasks, credited kills, streak rewards and confirmed paid cancellation/XP rewards. Dungeoneering retains its ordinary/challenge rooms, owner protection and exactly-once rewards; ordinary surface familiars are now suppressed during those trials. Attack, Strength, Defence and Constitution retain the original XP selections and basic loop; Magic retains automatic air-spell progression/rune costs; Necromancy retains its verified basic attack. This pass does not claim new rituals, prayer books, Slayer task expansions or generated dungeon floors. See `skills-secondpass-combat.md` and `skills-secondpass-production.md` for the remaining limitations of those paths.
